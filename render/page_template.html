Dream Fog Reel

Thirteen dreams, each turned from words into a short low-framerate clip and a still. The base footage should look like real footage. The fog on top depends on how clearly the dreamer says they remember it.

This sandbox has no image or video generation model, so the base footage is path-traced 3D (Blender Cycles, all procedural) with a true depth pass. The fog stage, the spec and the clip assembly are the parts meant to carry over to production, where a generative model replaces the 3D scenes.

New · fog tone

Dream fog can be black, and it can change partway through

The tone of the fog now comes from the dream itself. Dread, smoke, darkness and being chased push it toward black. Calm, warm and bright push it toward pale. Each beat of the dream ("then", "suddenly", a new sentence) is scored on its own, so a dream that turns can turn the fog with it. Dashed tags show the words that set the tone.

How a dream becomes a clip

Five stages

parse

Reads clarity words ("crystal clear" up, "hazy", "couldn't see" down, negation aware), style words (black and white, sepia, film, VHS, vivid), colour tints, setting, light, people and motion.

base footage

Builds one of nine 3D scenes and renders 14 to 22 frames along a slow camera path, plus a depth pass and the subject's screen position.

dream pass

Depth fog that lights glow through, a clear "attention island" around the subject, soft periphery, slow warping, bloom and halation, milky blacks, desaturation, grain.

styles

Only when named: mono with film black point, flicker and dust; noir; sepia; VHS chroma bleed; vivid; pastel; colour tints.

clip

6 to 12 fps with irregular holds, echo trails and double exposures between frames, fog still drifting under held frames, fade from fog and back.

0.55Fog half-distance about 3.5 times median depth, periphery softens, lights bloom, colour drops about 20%, 8 fps, echo trails.
0.90Fog closes in to about the median depth, edges dissolve into the fog colour, shapes wobble, 6 fps, heavy echo. Only the subject stays readable.
For production

What to swap, what to keep

Keep the dream pass, the spec and the clip assembly. Replace the 3D scenes with a text-to-image model (FLUX or SDXL class) prompted for plain photographic footage, then image-to-video (Veo, Kling, Runway or Wan class) for 4 to 6 seconds of slow camera, or a cheaper depth-based parallax. Run Depth Anything V2 on the generated frames to feed the fog. Let Claude fill the dream spec, with this rule-based parser as the offline fallback. Port the dream pass to a GPU shader so the app can preview fog changes live.
